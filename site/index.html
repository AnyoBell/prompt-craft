AI academic integrity

Ground rules before you touch a keyboard

+
  • No cheating. Where the line sits between getting help and getting AI to do the work for you.
  • AI as a tool for learning. Using it to check your reasoning, not replace it.
  • Hallucinations. Why AI invents facts confidently, and how to catch it before it costs you a grade.
02

How to structure a good prompt

The four pieces every strong prompt has

+
  • Subject and goal. Say what you're working on and what you actually need — clearly, not vaguely.
  • Limitations. Length, tone, reading level, format — spell out the constraints or the AI will guess.
  • Re-ups. Your first answer is a draft. Learn to push back and refine it.
  • Examples. Show the AI what "good" looks like instead of describing it.
03

What to ask

Turning AI into an actual study partner

+
  • Explanations. Getting a concept explained, not just defined.
  • Specific modes of explaining. Ask for an analogy, a diagram in words, a walkthrough — whatever fits the topic.
  • Adjustments for your learning style. Simpler, more visual, more technical — you can ask for all of it.
  • Summarization. Turning a dense reading into something you can actually hold onto.
  • Finding gaps in your own understanding. Using AI to test what you don't know yet, not just confirm what you do.
04

Studying with AI

Putting it all to work before an exam

+
  • Flashcards. Turning your notes into a deck AI generates and you actually use.
  • Study guides. Getting a structured guide out of scattered material.
  • Quizzes. Testing yourself with AI-generated questions before the real thing does.

Questions

Before you sign up

Is this against my school's AI policy?+

No — the first module is built around this exact question. You'll leave with a clear, defensible sense of what's allowed, and how to use AI in a way that holds up under any professor's policy.

Do I need any experience with AI tools?+

None. The course assumes you've maybe typed a question into a chatbot once and nothing more. Everything is built from there.

What do I need to bring?+

A laptop or phone with access to any free AI chat tool, and something you're currently studying — an actual class of yours makes the practice stick.

Is this a for-credit class?+

No — it's an independent workshop, not a credit-bearing course. It's built to be useful alongside whatever you're already taking.

What if I can't make all four sessions?+

Reach out before enrolling — recordings or a modified schedule may be possible depending on group size.
